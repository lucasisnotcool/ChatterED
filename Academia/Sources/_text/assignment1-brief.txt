1. Assignment at a glance

In this individual assignment, you will investigate one specific university learning task in
which students use generative AI but still experience a learning difficulty. You will identify
one evidence-supported difficulty, define a focused learning goal, decide what the learner
and the CUI should each do, choose a suitable CUI form and technical approach, and
develop a prototype of one core, multi-turn CUI learning interaction. Assignment 1 prepares
this interaction for implementation in Assignment 2. You will demonstrate and explain your
prototype in an individual video.

You will be assessed on the quality and justification of your decisions, how clearly your
prototype communicates the intended interaction, and whether you understand and can
explain what you submitted. You may use AI tools throughout the assignment. However, AI
may support rather than replace the responsibilities listed below.


How you may use AI
The following responsibilities remain yours when you use AI:
    1.​ You must personally obtain consent and talk to or observe at least three real adult
        university students. AI-generated or simulated participants do not count as learner
        evidence.
    2.​ You must make the final decisions about the learning problem, learning goal, CUI
        role, and technical approach.
    3.​ You must check that your prototype accurately represents the intended interaction
        and understand any code, prompts, configurations, and claims you submit.
    4.​ You must appear in the video and explain the project in your own words. An
        AI-generated avatar or voice cannot replace you.


What counts as a CUI?
A CUI is not limited to a text chatbot. In this assignment, a CUI is an interactive system in
which the learner and the system take turns communicating, and the system's next response
or action is shaped by what the learner says, does, or provides.

It may use text, voice, video, multimodal interaction, external tools, or agents. It may act as a
tutor, coach, practice partner, teachable peer, reflection partner, or another learning-oriented
role.

Note:

    1.​ Voice, video, multimodal, or agent-based systems do not receive automatic bonus
        marks compared to a text-only chatbot.
    2.​ Choose the form that best fits the learners, learning goal, and what you can build
        well.
    3.​ Using a larger, newer, or paid model is not, by itself, an innovation.

Examples for inspiration:

    ●​ StuBot asks learners to teach a topic in their own words and responds with questions
        about missing parts.
    ●​ Khanmigo acts as a coach that guides learners instead of simply giving them an
        answer.
    ●​ Duolingo Roleplay and Video Call provide conversational language practice
        followed by feedback.


2. What you need to do

Complete the following four steps in order. Use the same order in your report.
Step 1: Understand what remains difficult after AI
assistance
Interview at least 3 adult university students about one specific learning task (e.g.,
programming, reading, mathematics, abstraction, planning, and decomposition) for which
they currently use generative AI. You may ask them questions or observe how they use and
respond to the AI's help. Ask for their permission and do not include names or other
identifying information in your submission.

Summarise what you learned in four parts:

   1.​ Learner and task: Who (major? Grad? … etc) are you designing for, what are they
       trying to learn, and how do they currently use AI?
   2.​ Current difficulty: After receiving AI assistance, what does the learner still struggle
       to understand, or do for themselves?

       Choose one of the following difficulties that best describes your target learner, or
       identify another difficulty supported by your learner evidence:

           ●​ Reduced persistence: When the task becomes difficult, the learner turns to
               AI before making or sustaining a meaningful attempt of their own.
           ●​ Uncritical use of AI output: The learner can obtain an AI-assisted answer
               but struggles to explain, question, verify, or improve it using their own
               reasoning.
           ●​ Reduced ownership and self-direction: The learner relies on AI to determine
               the approach or next steps and struggles to make key decisions for
               themselves.
           ●​ Other: A different difficulty that emerged from your interviews or
               observations and is not adequately captured by the categories above. Give it a
               clear name and definition.

       For your chosen difficulty, describe how it appears in the specific learning task you
       are studying. Explain what the learner can accomplish with AI assistance and what
       they still struggle to understand or do on their own. Your choice must be supported
       by evidence from your learner interviews or observations.

   3.​ User Needs: Summarize key needs identified from target user interviews
   4.​ Potential CUI support: How a CUI could assist them in overcoming these obstacles,
       and how it can empower them to master these tasks independently.
Step 2: Decide the learning goal and the CUI's role
Describe your plan in the following parts:

   1.​ Learning goal: Based on the difficulty identified in Step 1, state one specific thing
       the learner should understand or learn to do. Explain what the learner would explain
       or do to show progress toward this goal.
   2.​ Roles of the learner and the CUI: Clearly state what the learner must think about or
       do themselves during the learning activity, and what the CUI will do to support that
       work without taking it over.
   3.​ Why this approach: What does conversational interaction add to this learning
       process? Could a simpler non-conversational design work just as well?
   4.​ Design Goals: Define at least three clear design goals grounded in relevant theories
       and identified user needs, providing a solid justification for each.


Step 3: Implementation Plan
Detail the CUI model and tech stack (such as a chatbot, hybrid CUI+GUI, multimodal
framework, rule-driven logic, or prompt engineering) chosen for your functional prototype,
explaining how these options directly support the target learning challenge, learning
objective, and planned interaction model.

Outline your implementation strategy. While implementation is not required for Assignment
1, and prototyping tools are permitted, you must clearly demonstrate your technical plan and
understanding:

   ●​ What components need to be developed?
   ●​ Which parts will you build yourself?
   ●​ Which aspects will rely on an LLM for development assistance?

Provide a clear overview of the system architecture and justify your approach to leveraging
AI to build the conversational interface. Keep in mind that technical complexity alone does
not translate to higher marks. A straightforward CUI can earn top marks if it addresses
student challenges effectively and accurately. Ultimately, success hinges on sound design
decisions rather than reliance on complex or cutting-edge technologies.
Step 4: Develop a CUI prototype
Develop a prototype of one multi-turn CUI learning interaction based on your work in Steps
1–3. The interaction must involve several connected exchanges between the learner and the
CUI as they work toward the learning goal. Assignment 1 focuses on designing and
prototyping this interaction to be ready for implementation, not on building a CUI system.
You may use code, digital prototyping tools, storyboards, paper materials, or another suitable
prototyping method. Whatever method you choose, the prototype must be detailed enough
to demonstrate how the interaction works and how it addresses one key learning need
identified in Step 1.

Your prototype does not need to cover every planned feature, but it must:

   1.​ Enable the learner to engage in and complete a single core learning activity across
       multiple conversational turns.
   2.​ Show what the learner thinks about or does, and how the CUI's responses are shaped
       by the learner's input across the interaction.
   3.​ Show how the interaction addresses the identified learning need and supports the
       learning goal from Step 2.

In your demonstration video, explicitly show how the interaction works:

   4.​ Walk through the interface to illustrate the system operations and expected user
       interactions (imperfections in an initial prototype are completely fine).
   5.​ Roleplay or act out the step-by-step user interaction to show how the system is
       intended to function.

4. How your work will be assessed

Your Assignment 1 mark is out of 16 and counts directly toward the 100-mark course total.
Graders will assess the four areas below.

Learning Problem and User Needs (4.0 marks)​
To demonstrate a deep understanding of the problem space and target user group, base your
submission on primary empirical evidence collected through interviews or observational
sessions with at least 3 adult university students. Identify their specific academic program,
gender, or year of study rather than names or IDs to demonstrate relevance to your chosen
topic. You must clearly outline a focused learning task and examine how students currently
incorporate generative AI tools into their study workflow. Pinpoint a primary challenge
faced by learners, supporting your findings with direct user quotes, observed behaviors, or
documented responses. Conclude by articulating the fundamental user need your CUI design
intends to address.

Learning Design and Chosen Approach (4.0 marks)​
This section requires a well-defined learning objective alongside a clear mechanism for
tracking student progress. You should establish an explicit division of responsibilities
between the student and the CUI, while providing a compelling rationale for adopting a
conversational modality. Your design goals must be rooted in established communication
and learning theories as well as empirical user needs. Furthermore, present a feasible,
well-suited technical implementation strategy and CUI framework, ensuring every design
choice and architectural decision is thoroughly justified. To ground your approach, draw
upon lecture materials, theoretical foundations, and relevant scholarly literature from the
course reading list or external academic sources.

Please properly attribute or cite these resources in your report, so then we will be able to
know how you grounded your design properly.

CUI Prototype and Interaction (3.0 marks)​
Construct a low-fidelity prototype illustrating a multi-turn CUI learning exchange. The
design must foster active cognitive engagement from the learner, offer tailored feedback,
guidance, or progressive steps following user input, and directly address the target learning
challenge. Evaluation centers on the clarity and coherence with which the interaction design
is communicated. Suitable medium formats encompass low-complexity solutions such as
conversation flows, paper-based mockups, scripts, storyboards, or Dialogflow setups.
Selection of the appropriate prototyping approach is left to your discretion.

The purpose of these rapid prototyping techniques is to clarify the core concept and prepare
it for implementation and user testing in Assignment 2. High visual fidelity is not expected;
assessment focuses on the quality and clarity of the interaction design rather than its visual
presentation.

Individual Video and Ownership (5.0 marks)​
A clear demonstration of the complete, multi-turn prototype interaction; project-specific
explanation and reasoning; and clear evidence that the student understands and owns the
submitted work. Within this criterion, 2 marks are for the demonstration, 2 marks are for
project-specific explanation and reasoning, and 1 mark is for individual understanding and
ownership.
