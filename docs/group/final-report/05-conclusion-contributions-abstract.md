## Abstract

Students who use AI chat for homework usually send the question without their own attempt, so
the help that comes back cannot fit what they already know. We present ChatterED, an app that
turns a student's own homework into a workspace with an answer slot under every part, where each
request for help carries the student's attempt and code decides how much help is given. The
design rests on eight interviews and six shared AI study sessions. In a field study, four
undergraduates used the app unsupervised; 15 of the 21 parts they touched began with their own
attempt, and 9 of the 14 that fell short at the first check were later repaired. The study also
showed that the model's marking can be wrong against the student, who has no way to contest it.

## 8. Limitations and conclusion

Section 6.6 gave the limits of the study's results, and two further limits apply to the project
as a whole. Firstly, the coding in both the user research and the user study was carried out by
AI models, and although we reviewed the results, no person coded the data independently.
Secondly, the app runs only on a Mac and depends on a paid model, which narrowed who could take
part.

ChatterED set out to bring the student's own attempt into the conversation with an AI tutor, and
to take the work of steering that conversation away from the student. In a small field study the
first aim held, as students wrote before they asked. The second held only in part, since students still had to argue with a verdict that was
wrong and to work out what two agents were for. The next version would therefore let a student
contest a verdict, tell the agent what has changed since the last check, use a single agent, and
give a student who has nothing yet to reason from something to start with. Whether this leads
students to learn more is a question that needs a comparison condition and a measure of learning,
which our study did not have.
