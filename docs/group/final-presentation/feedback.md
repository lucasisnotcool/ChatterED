The following comment integrates the feedback from the teaching team:

This was a great presentation, and you handled the questions really well. You showed a strong, in-depth understanding of the problem. The finding that none of 31 messages students sent to general AI chatbots included their own attempt gave ChatterED a clear purpose, and we like how it guides students back to the lecture material. The three versions of the tutorial sheet were easy to follow, and fixing Max, your checking agent, so it follows the answer key was a good call. Your demo was clear, complete and engaging, and watching it catch a wrong answer and accept the fix was satisfying. Your plan to recruit target users to work with the system while they prepare for their final exams is realistic and good, and your week-long study states its limits clearly. Your choice of qualitative analysis also suits exploring users' experiences. On how students know what they can do alone, your answer was specific and honest about dropping the before-and-after test.

It is very important to revise your research questions, which are not yet clear. In your second question, "what students do next" is vague, and it does not say whether what they do is good or bad, so explain the dependent variables you want to look into. Say more clearly what learning outcome you are looking for, and align your measurements more closely with it. Explain how analysing the AI's responses helps answer your research questions. For the conversation analysis, you could also use inductive coding alongside the deductive ICAP coding, as we said in the Q&A, to learn more from the student and AI interactions. Recruit your STEM participants soon, since your participant description leaves STEM out.

In the final report, give more detail on where your design approaches came from. The pilot fixes were not tested again with users, so check whether the three problems still appear. No hint appeared in the demo, so add an example to the report of a student asking for one and getting a step, not the answer. As we discussed at the mid-term, consider reducing the complexity of your system so that it is easier to follow, and spend a little more time explaining each function in the video you submit.

For future talks, use a larger font, especially for system screenshots, and make your slides less dense.
